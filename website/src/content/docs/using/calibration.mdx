A page printer rarely prints exactly where the page says. The paper moves a little in the tray, the print is a fraction too large or too small, and a sheet of labels leaves no room for that: a print that is half a millimetre off already touches the edge of a label. **Calibration** measures how far a tray is off. Labelyard then moves every print from that tray by the opposite.

You do this once per printer and tray, and again when you change the paper or the tray. It takes about ten minutes. Open **Calibration** in the web app and choose the printer, the tray and the sheet type at the top. The page has four tabs: **Print page** (step 1), **Readings** (step 2), **Result** (step 3, the stored values and the check page) and **History**. A tray with a stored correction opens on **Result**, any other on **Print page**.

## What you need

- The page printer, added as a **Page printer (IPP)** with its trays. See [Printers](/install/printers/#page-printers).
- A real sheet of the sheet type you will print on. Print on the sheet itself, not on plain paper: the cuts of the labels are what you measure against.
- The correct **sheet type**. If its measurements are wrong, the calibration cannot be right; see [Sheets and layouts](/using/sheets/).

## Step 1: Print the calibration page

Choose the printer, the tray and the sheet type, then press **Print calibration page** (or download the PDF and print it yourself). The page shows the cut lines of the sheet type and a small scale at every crossing between four labels.

The page starts on the tray your sheets last printed from. Calibrate that tray; the page warns if the calibration page came from another one.

Writing a number on each cell, row by row, is optional. It helps when you take photos of the sheet.

## Step 2: Enter the deviation

### One crossing

<div class="ly-guide-figure">
<Crossing role="img" aria-label="How to read one crossing" />
</div>

1. The two edges of the gap between two columns: the sides of the labels.
2. The middle of that gap (dash-dot line). The printed line should lie here.
3. The cut between two rows.

- Heavy solid line: the printed **S line**. Heavy dashed line: the printed **Z line** (dotted on a sheet without a gap between the rows, as in the drawing). S counts the gaps between columns, Z the gaps between rows.
- The numbers on the scale are the value to enter when the middle of the gap (across) or the cut (down) falls there.
- The scale lies on both lines: 0 on the printed line, a long tick every 1 mm, a short tick every 0.5 mm, up to ±2 mm.
- Example: the middle of the gap lies 0.6 mm left of the printed line and the cut 0.4 mm above it. The scale reads +0.6 and +0.4 there, so across is +0.6 and down is +0.4.

At a crossing, look where the middle of the gap (across) or the cut (down) falls on the scale, and count its distance from the printed line. Between two ticks, estimate to 0.1 mm by eye. The signs are:

- **+ across** = the ink lies right of the middle of the gap, **− across** = left of it.
- **+ down** = the ink lies below the cut, **− down** = above it.

If the sheet has no gap between the rows, the Z line sits on the cut. Peel off every other label (checkerboard) only if the cut is hard to see.

### Which crossings

Read at least the four outer crossings and the one in the centre. The drawing shows them for a sheet with 4 columns and 12 rows; the web app draws the one of your sheet type.

<div class="ly-guide-figure ly-guide-sheet">
<SheetCrossings role="img" aria-label="The crossings to read on a sheet with 4 columns and 12 rows" />
</div>

1. S1 · Z1
2. S3 · Z1
3. S1 · Z11
4. S3 · Z11
5. S2 · Z6 (centre)

The numbers in the drawing follow this list. S counts the gaps between columns from the left, Z the gaps between rows from the top.

### Enter the readings

Enter the readings under **Readings**:

1. **Readings from print** names the page the readings come from, by the run number printed in its margin. The newest print of this printer, tray and type is chosen for you, a calibration page or a check pass. For a check print, choose its pass of the check sheet ("Check sheet of run #12 — pass 2, run #14"): the open sheet, or the last one once its final pass is printed.
2. For each reading, choose the crossing (every S and Z line of the sheet is offered), then for each direction where the ink lies: **Left of the gap** or **Right of the gap**, **Above the cut** or **Below the cut**. The buttons set the sign, so you type only the size. A typed minus works too: `-0,4` picks left (or above) and leaves 0.4.
3. Type the size, or use **−** and **+** (or the arrow keys). They move by 0.1 mm, or by one tick of the printed scale (0.5 mm) when you choose that step. A value with more decimals is rounded to 0.1 mm, and the field says so ("You typed 0,43 … saved as 0.4 mm"). A reading past the end of the scale (±2.0 mm) is warned about: then check the tray, the sheet type or the printer.

From three readings the **live fit** shows the offset, scale and rotation and what is left at each crossing, 0.4 s after your last change. A crossing that is more than 0.3 mm off the fit is marked **probable misread**; read it again. Press **Fit** to fill the values in below. Nothing is saved yet: check the values, then save them. The save keeps the readings and the print they came from in the history.

The readings of a check print show what is left with the stored correction; Fit shows them but does not fill them in, and the hub never saves them as a correction. **Readings from print** starts at the newest print of this tray and sheet type, so after a check print its pass is chosen for you.

On a sheet with only two columns or two rows all crossings lie on one line, so the readings cannot be fitted and the readings helper is not offered. Enter the values directly in the form below it.

## Step 3: Check the result

Print the check page. It is the same page, now with the correction. If every crossing is within ±0.5 mm, you are done. If not, measure again and adjust.

One sheet of labels serves up to three check prints. Before a check print, Labelyard asks which sheet you print on:

- **Print on the same sheet (pass 2 of 3)**: put the sheet of the last check back into the tray. This pass prints only its own crosses, each marked `K2` or `K3`, at crossings that never touch the earlier ones, plus one short line with its run number where the margin has room.
- **New sheet**: pass 1 on a fresh sheet. It is the full check page with its cell numbers and codes, and its crosses are marked `K1`.

The dialog says how many passes the sheet takes ("This sheet takes 2 check passes"). It also says before printing what the page will lack on this printer: fewer than four page codes, margin text that does not fit, or later passes without their run line. A sheet type with fewer than five crossings takes one pass, the full check page.

When you enter the readings of a later pass, enter the crossings marked with that pass's `K` label.

## Printer or sheet type?

When the page is still off, the deviation tells you where to look. Compare what you see with this table:

<div class="ly-causes">

| What you see | Cause | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| The same shift in one direction everywhere, on every sheet type | Offset of the printer or tray | Calibrate; the correction removes it |
| The deviation grows with the distance, on every sheet type, possibly by different amounts across and down | Scale of the printer (it prints too large or too small) | Calibrate |
| A vertical line drifts sideways towards the bottom, or the rows tilt (one side higher than the other) | Rotation (the printer prints skewed) | Calibrate |
| The same shift or a drift on one sheet type only, while other sheets sit with the same correction | Measurements of the sheet type (margin, pitch, label height) | Correct the sheet type (add a new version), not the printer |
| A jump between two neighbouring gaps | Sheet type (gap, number of columns or rows) | Correct the sheet type |
| A sideways shift that differs from sheet to sheet | Paper feed | Push the sheet against the guide and print a second one; change the correction only if it repeats |
| An even remainder below about 0.2 mm | Reading accuracy | Do nothing |

</div>

The rule of thumb for what is left after the fit:

- equal everywhere: printer offset;
- growing evenly in both directions: printer scale;
- growing evenly across the gaps of one type only: that type's pitch.

**The caveat:** on a calibration page the fit takes all three in, because a pitch error grows evenly with the gap like a scale error, and a margin error is a shift like an offset. It only shows on a **check print** of the type, made with a correction that was measured on **another** sheet type.

### Geometry suggestion

When you enter the readings of such a check print, the readings helper suggests new values for the type, for example "Pitch across 48.50 → 48.20 mm". If the readings follow no straight line it says **uneven**: check the sheet, not the printer. When there is no suggestion it says why: the correction was measured on the same type, it is not known which type it was measured on, or the check print was made without a correction (then it shows the printer, not the sheet; print the check page with the correction).

An admin can press **Apply as new version**: Labelyard adds a version of the sheet type with these values and a change note naming the print, and opens the prefilled feedback issue on GitHub so the catalogue can be corrected too. Sheets already made keep their version. **Fill the feedback issue** opens the same issue without changing anything.

A wrong pitch does not show as a jump but as a steady drift, much like a printer scale error. That is why the sheet types matter: if one type drifts while the others sit with the same correction, look at that type. One real case was a sheet type whose row pitch was 9.98 instead of 10.0 mm.

The check print follows the same rule: every crossing within ±0.5 mm is done. A remainder that is larger, or that grows towards one edge, is one of the rows above.

### Reading tips

- Read across and down separately at every crossing, each as a number. "Looks centred" is not a reading.
- Read crossings in the first, middle and last row and column; otherwise a drift stays hidden.
- If the scale is hard to read, the known gap width (column pitch minus label width) works as a ruler.
- Vendor data sheets can be off. Measure a pitch with a ruler over ten labels and divide by ten.
- For photos: full resolution, zoom into each crossing, and low-angle light makes the cut visible. Bent paper makes lines look curved, so judge at the crossing only.

## Probe print on plain paper

Before you spend a sheet of labels, you can see on plain paper whether a sheet type fits. The **probe print** draws the outline of every label of the type with its number and its row and column (`R2 C3`), the preprinted areas hatched and the place of the sheet code dashed. It uses the tray's current correction. Hold the page against a real sheet in front of a window or a lamp: when the outlines sit on the cuts, the sheet type and the correction are right.

Open it with **Probe print on plain paper** on a sheet type in the [sheet archive](/using/sheets/), or next to **Print calibration page** on the **Print page** tab. Choose the printer and the tray and press **Print probe page**, or **Download PDF** to print it yourself. No sheet is used and no cell is reserved. The page goes through the print queue like any other: you find it under **Jobs**, where you can cancel or retry it, and it takes the tray's next run number, so **History** lists it with the calibration pages.

The dialog says before printing when the printer's latest status says it is offline (switch it on or choose another printer) and when the hub sees a different device behind the printer: a probe page is not printed until an admin confirms the device, because the stored correction would be wrong for it.

### Photograph the measurement sheet

The **measurement sheet** is the probe print with a millimetre grid over the whole page: a line every millimetre, a heavier one every 5 mm and a number every 10 mm, counted from the top-left corner of the page. It is drawn **without** correction, so the grid shows the page's own millimetres. Use it to find out where a vendor's preprint (a logo, a printed border, a text field) sits on a sheet:

1. Choose **Measurement sheet** on the sheet type, or tick **Measurement sheet** in the dialog.
2. Print it **on a real sheet** of the type, not on plain paper, so the preprint and the grid end up on the same page.
3. Photograph the sheet straight from above, at full resolution, with even light. Zoom in on the edges of each preprinted area and read their positions off the grid.
4. Enter what you read as preprinted areas of the sheet type (see [Sheets and layouts](/using/sheets/)).

The footer of the page says "Measurement sheet — no correction". On sheet types whose margins are too narrow for a line of text (such as Avery 3474, 3490 or 5159), that footer, and the run number and time, are printed inside the first label cell that lies in the printable area instead. Expect them there in the photo.

## History

Every save is kept. The **History** tab lists the corrections of the tray, newest first, with the one in use now marked. Each entry shows its values, how much each one changed against the entry before (**vs. before**), where the values came from (a fit from readings of a numbered print, typed by hand, or restored from an earlier entry), the sheet type it was measured on and an optional note. The correction a tray had when you upgraded to 1.8.0 is the first entry, marked **Imported**. Labelyard keeps the newest 500 entries per printer and tray.

**N jobs printed with it** opens the jobs list with only the jobs printed with that correction.

**Restore these values** (admins only) uses an earlier entry again. The confirmation names the values and takes an optional note. Restoring adds a new entry; nothing is overwritten or deleted.

Below the corrections, **Pages printed from this tray** lists every calibration, check and probe page with its run number. A retry keeps the number of its run and says **(retry)**; a page that was never handed to the printer is marked **not submitted**. **Printed from** is the tray the printer reported. When it is not the tray you asked for, the tab warns at the top: a correction measured on such a page belongs to the tray the page really came from. Some printers do not report it; the column then says so.
